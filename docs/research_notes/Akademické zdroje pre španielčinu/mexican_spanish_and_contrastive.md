# Mexican Spanish and Slovak/Czech–Spanish contrastive linguistics: source catalogue

Method note: ~17 searches/fetches. Items marked VERIFIED were seen on a catalogue, publisher or journal page in this session. Items marked UNVERIFIED come from background knowledge or an unconfirmed search snippet. Do not cite UNVERIFIED items without checking them first. Several of the requested authors and titles could NOT be confirmed (see Gaps).

## Part A. Mexican Spanish: standard descriptions, usage points, institutions, ELE materials

### Takeaway
The backbone for Mexican Spanish is El Colegio de México (Colmex) and UNAM output. These include Lope Blanch's Atlas lingüístico de México, the Barriga/Martín Butragueño Historia sociolingüística de México, Company's Sintaxis histórica and Moreno Fernández's variety-teaching works. Many NRFH and Anuario de Letras articles are free online. Specific usage points (pretérito vs. compuesto, diminutives, ahorita) have dedicated Mexico-focused studies. I found no Mexico-specific scholarship for some of the others (¿mande?, ándale/órale, ya/no más). Treat those as gaps.

### Cited Findings

#### A1. Standard descriptions
- Lope Blanch, J. M. (dir.), *Atlas lingüístico de México*, Colmex / UNAM / FCE, published 1990–2000. Co-authors: A. Alcalá Alba, G. Cantero Sandoval, J. López Chávez, A. Millán Orozco, J. G. Moreno de Alba. Authority: lexical and phonetic geography of Mexico, for dialect and lexicon claims. Print, in libraries; some Colmex repository items are online. App use: check whether a word is general Mexican or regional before labelling it "Mexican". — [NRFH review](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/2513/2493/2570); [Colmex repository](https://repositorio.colmex.mx/downloads/4f16c357m?locale=es)

His essay "El español de América y la norma lingüística hispánica" is cited in reviews. There is NO confirmed book titled exactly "El español de México" by Lope Blanch in my results. — [NRFH](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/2513/2493/2570)
- Moreno de Alba, J. G., *Notas de gramática dialectal (el Atlas Lingüístico de México)*, UNAM, 2013. It reads most of the Atlas maps in detail. Authority: dialect grammar. — [same source](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/2513/2493/2570)
- Barriga Villanueva, R. and Martín Butragueño, P. (dirs.), *Historia sociolingüística de México*, Colmex. Volume 3 includes Martín Butragueño's "La división dialectal del español mexicano". The Colmex repository marks vol. 1 as restricted, so this is mostly paid or library access. Page counts for vol. 2 differ between reviews, so check the catalogue. Authority: the dialect division of Mexican Spanish and its sociolinguistic history. — [NRFH review](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/1041/1038); [Colmex repository](https://repositorio.colmex.mx/concern/books/kp78gk30f?locale=en)
- Company Company, C. (dir.), *Sintaxis histórica de la lengua española*, UNAM / FCE, 3 parts, 7 volumes, 2006–2014. Part 1 covers the verb phrase (tenses and moods). Part 2 (2009) covers the noun phrase. Part 3 (2014) covers prepositions, adverbs, conjunctions and interclausal relations. Company is a member of the Academia Mexicana de la Lengua since 2004. Authority: history and Mexican/American syntax of tense, mood and prepositions. App use: backing for "why Mexican uses X". Part of the project is on UNAM's site. — [BVFE](https://www.bvfe.es/en/autor/21590-company-concepcion/imprimir.html); [UNAM divulgación](https://divulgacionpapiit.unam.mx/node/111)

Heidelberg's publication list gives 2009 and the Torrossa catalogue gives 2010, so the year conflicts. Authority: which variety to teach. Paid or print. Earlier related title: *¿Qué español enseñar?* (Arco/Libros, 2000). — [Heidelberg list](https://backend.hcias.uni-heidelberg.de/en/documents/list-of-publicationsfmf/download); [Torrossa](https://www.torrossa.com/en/resources/an/2966361)

#### A2. Usage points with Mexican scholarship
- Pretérito vs. compuesto: Lope Blanch, "Sobre el uso del pretérito en el español de México" (1961; reprinted 1972, a classic reference). García Fajardo studied the semantic value of the compound form in Mexican Spanish in NRFH. Spitzová and Bayerová (1987) treat the position of the compound perfect in the Mexican verb system. A 2019 Colmex dissertation studies four past tenses in Mexican Spanish, synchronically and diachronically. Hoduláková has a thesis comparing the Spain and Mexican preterites (Czech repository). — [search hits via UNAM ELA](https://ela.enallt.unam.mx/index.php/ela/article/download/599/651); [Colmex dissertation](https://repositorio.colmex.mx/downloads/np1939649?locale=es); [Hoduláková thesis (Masaryk)](https://digilib.phil.muni.cz/cs/_flysystem/fedora/pdf/113420.pdf). The full texts were not opened, so the exact bibliographic data need checking. App use: backs the app's "pretérito by default, compuesto rare/resultative" rule.
- Diminutives: Słowik, "Aspectos semántico-pragmáticos de los diminutivos en el español de la Ciudad de México", *Studia Iberystyczne* (based on Corpus Sociolingüístico de la Ciudad de México; year as given by the search was 2017/2018, uncertain). Pechancová (2019), a master's thesis at the University of South Bohemia on diminutives in Mexican Spanish. Also an article on Nahuatl influence on diminutive use (UNAM, *Estudios de Cultura Náhuatl*). — [Słowik](https://journals.akademicka.pl/si/article/view/182); [Pechancová record](https://invenio.nusl.cz/record/395036); [UNAM Nahuatl article](https://ru.historicas.unam.mx/handle/20.500.12525/9330)
- ahorita: Granados, D., "Atenuación y expansión de ahorita en la Ciudad de México, un cambio analizado desde el tiempo aparente", *Cuadernos de Lingüística de El Colegio de México*, vol. 8 (free). Malaver, I. (2017), "Ahorita": lexicalización y cambio lingüístico en la comunidad de habla caraqueña, NRFH 65(1), 27–57, DOI 10.24201/nrfh.v65i1.2828, is about Caracas and is only comparative. — [Cuadernos de Lingüística](https://cuadernoslinguistica.colmex.mx/index.php/cl/article/download/202/314/2076); [NRFH](https://revistas.colmex.mx/Record/oai:oai.nrfh.colmex.mx:article-2828)
- Not found (no Mexican scholarly source located): ¿mande?, ándale/órale (intensifier le), ya/no más, ir a + infinitive futurity, subjunctive patterns, usted vs. tú pragmatics in Mexico, Mexican vs. Spain lexicon beyond the Atlas.

#### A3. Institutions
- UNAM CEPE offers the in-house exams EPLE, CELA (diagnostic, valid two years) and EXELEAA, plus SIELE (co-developed with Instituto Cervantes, Salamanca and UBA). In 2024 it applied 1,157 in-person SIELE exams. The journal *Decires* (CEPE) has an article comparing the exams. CEPE also trains teachers (EEELE, DIPELE). CEPE textbook titles were NOT found. — [CEPE annual report 2024](https://web.planeacion.unam.mx/Memoria/2024/PDF/14.9-CEPE.pdf); [Decires article](https://decires.cepe.unam.mx/index.php/decires/article/download/291/259/512)
- Colmex (Centro de Estudios Lingüísticos y Literarios): repository at repositorio.colmex.mx. Journals NRFH and Cuadernos de Lingüística are mostly open access.
- Academia Mexicana de la Lengua, UNAM Instituto de Investigaciones Filológicas, Tec de Monterrey Spanish programmes: no pages verified.

### Inferences
- Pretérito/compuesto and diminutive claims in tips can be anchored to Lope Blanch 1961 and the CDMX corpus studies. The other pragmatic particles (mande, órale, ándale) may need to be written as "observed usage" and checked with native speakers, because no scholarly source was found.
- The Atlas and Colmex repository are the best free checks for "is this Mexican".

## Part B. Slovak/Czech–Spanish contrast

### Takeaway
Slovak hispanistics is centred on Comenius University (Katedra romanistiky). The best verified figure is Bohdan Ulašin, whose works are mostly lexical. Learner-error work for Czech and Slovak learners exists. The key title is Rodríguez García (2021, Masaryk UP). Several names and titles in the brief (Hamplová's "Mluvnice", Prokopová's "Učebnice") were corrected or not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Rodríguez García (2021) is the best single source for typical Czech/Slovak error categories (articles, tenses, ser/estar, lexicon). Fetch the table of contents from Masaryk University Press before anything is cited from it.
- Czech repositories (theses.cz, dspace.cuni.cz, dspace.jcu.cz) are free and searchable, so they are the cheapest way to back tips on false friends and interference. Treat theses as weaker evidence than journal articles.
