## 1. Spanish-specific L2 acquisition (ser/estar, preterite/imperfect, subjunctive, clitics, gender, por/para, articles, phonology)

### Takeaway
Five items are bibliographically confirmed: VanPatten's ser/estar stages (via secondary sources), Geeslin 2003 on copula choice, Salaberry/Andersen-Shirai on aspect, Collentine's subjunctive work (Hispania, not SSLA), and VanPatten & Cadierno 1993 on object pronouns. For the app, the evidence supports (a) teaching ser/estar with estar + adjective last, (b) treating preterite/imperfect as a late-mastered contrast with a tendency to overuse the preterite, (c) not expecting explicit instruction alone to deliver the subjunctive, and (d) using input-first (structured input) practice for clitics. Gender agreement, por/para, articles for Slavic learners and L2 Spanish phonology could not be sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- VanPatten (1987) is cited as describing five developmental stages of ser/estar acquisition (earlier version 1985), starting with omission of the copula and its overgeneralization; the last stage reached is estar with adjectives. This is secondhand, from a later summary. The original article was not seen; its exact title and journal are UNVERIFIED here (background memory: "The acquisition of ser and estar by adult learners of Spanish: A preliminary investigation of transfer" or a similar title, do not cite until checked). — [search result summarising stages, hispadoc](https://hispadoc.es/descarga/articulo/2371786.pdf); [VanPatten 2010 essay PDF (Weber State course copy)](https://faculty.weber.edu/tmathews/SLI/Readings/VanPatten%202010.pdf)
- VanPatten (2010) reanalyses earlier ser/estar research (VanPatten 1985, 1987) with current linguistic theory and locates the difficulty in acquiring estar and its perfective nature. Published in Hispania per search summary (volume and pages not confirmed). — [Weber State copy](https://faculty.weber.edu/tmathews/SLI/Readings/VanPatten%202010.pdf)
- Perpiñán (2019) proposes a revised developmental path building on VanPatten (1987), arguing learners find the [+dynamic] feature with nominal subjects hardest; the repository record is of a related study on aspect in the acquisition of ser/estar in locative contexts by English-speaking learners. — [UIC repository](https://repositori.uic.es/handle/20.500.12328/2864)
- Geeslin (2003) compared contextualised questionnaire data from advanced learners with native speakers and found semantic and pragmatic features interact to predict estar; they differ between groups. Journal cited as Language Learning (volume and pages not shown in the result: UNVERIFIED). Geeslin (2005), *Crossing Disciplinary Boundaries to Improve the Analysis of Second Language Data* (LINCOM, LSLA 15) is the book-length version. Her argument: learner copula choice should be modelled with statistical predictors, not error analysis, because obligatory contexts are unclear in native Spanish too. — [Arizona dissertation record 1999](https://repository.arizona.edu/handle/10150/288962); [LINCOM shop](https://lincom-shop.eu/LSLA-15-Crossing-Disciplinary-Boundaries-to-Improve-the-Analysis-of-Second-Language-Data); [Cambridge BLC article "From theory to research: contextual predictors of estar + adjective"](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/bilingualism-language-and-cognition/article/from-theory-to-research-contextual-predictors-of-estar-adjective-and-the-study-of-the-sla-of-spanish-copula-choice/B4B22BB3C5A83E29C1AF1D5B85C85D55)
- Geeslin & Gudmestad (2010), range and frequency of forms in variable structures in L2 Spanish, *Studies in Second Language Acquisition* 32, 433–463 (page range from a reference list). — [Geeslin author page on scite](https://scite.ai/authors/kimberly-l-geeslin-V0Zk4j)
- Aspect Hypothesis / Lexical Aspect Hypothesis: Andersen & Shirai (1994, 1996) are the standard citations; inherent verb aspect guides early tense-aspect morphology (perfective with telic, imperfective with atelic). For Spanish, Salaberry (1999, 2002, 2008) and Quesada (2006) argue against it; Ramsay (1990) and Sánchez-Quintana (2005) support it. Salaberry's "Default Past Tense Hypothesis": English-L1 learners recently taught preterite/imperfect tend to overgeneralise the preterite. A separate study found dynamicity, not telicity, drives early use of past forms. Evidence is contested and partly methodological. Full citations of the original Andersen & Shirai and Salaberry papers were not retrieved. — [Benjamins TAML2 volume](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/dujal.19002.qui); [Cambridge BLC article on dynamic contrasts](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/product/040C44F2897AD21452E17E47414E2F98/core-reader); [Salaberry (2011) type of study, via search](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/product/B093435818E7EBFCF88CB71B98446CB8/core-reader)
- Salaberry & Shirai (2002), *The L2 Acquisition of Tense-Aspect Morphology* (John Benjamins) appears in reference lists; details not retrieved. — same Benjamins link above (listed in reference lists only)
- Comajoan: nothing found in this pass (UNVERIFIED, background only: Comajoan has studies on narrative discourse and tense-aspect in Catalan and Spanish L2).
- Collentine, J. (1995). The development of complex syntax and mood-selection abilities by intermediate-level learners of Spanish. *Hispania* 78, 122–135. Finding: intermediate learners seem unable to master mood selection (indicative/subjunctive) by the end of intermediate instruction. — [typeset.io PDF of the paper](https://typeset.io/pdf/the-development-of-complex-syntax-and-mood-selection-3wu6t65nmw.pdf)
- Collentine, J. (2010). The acquisition and teaching of the Spanish subjunctive: An update on current findings. *Hispania* 93(1), 39–51. Reviews research since his 2003 review (UG, psycholinguistic views, study abroad) and gives pedagogical recommendations. — [NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/the-acquisition-and-teaching-of-the-spanish-subjunctive-an-update/); [Weber State copy](https://faculty.weber.edu/tmathews/SLI/Readings/Collentine%202010.pdf)
- Collentine, J. (1998), "Processing instruction and the subjunctive" (venue not confirmed in the results; background memory suggests Hispania 81). Abstract: learners completing the intermediate level benefit little from subjunctive instruction. — [Weber State copy](https://faculty.weber.edu/tmathews/sli/Readings/Collentine%201998.pdf)
- Collentine, "Subjunctive in second language Spanish" in Geeslin (Ed.) 2013, *Handbook of Spanish SLA* (Wiley-Blackwell): instructional interventions have not proven particularly effective for subjunctive abilities. — [NAU record](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/subjunctive-in-second-language-spanish/)
- Gudmestad on the subjunctive: not found (UNVERIFIED).
- VanPatten, B., & Cadierno, T. (1993). Explicit instruction and input processing. *Studies in Second Language Acquisition* 15, 225–243. DOI 10.1017/S0272263100011979. Target: Spanish object pronouns and word order. Processing instruction (explanation plus input practice) gave gains in comprehension and production; traditional output-based instruction gave gains in production only. — [Cambridge Core PDF](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S0272263100011979); [todoele summary](https://todoele.net/node/445111). Caution: the Weber State file named "VanPatten & Cadierno 1993" is a different paper (Modern Language Journal 77(1), 45–57).
- VanPatten & Oikkenon (1996), Explanation versus structured input in processing instruction (SSLA): benefits came from the structured input activities, not the explicit explanation. — [Cambridge Core](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/explanation-versus-structured-input-in-processing-instruction/CADC0357472A2FF7A8195A3A58A8E602) (year and conclusion from the search summary)

### Inferences
- Ser/estar: teach ser (identity, origin, profession, characteristics) and estar (location, then states) separately, and put estar + adjective (the ser/estar contrast with the same adjective) late, since it is the last stage reached. Keep ser vs. estar minimal-pair drills (matching, find the mistake) for B1, after the separate uses are solid. Expect omission and overuse errors early, and store each as a Chyby item.
- Preterite/imperfect: the evidence on lexical aspect is mixed, so do not build the order on the Aspect Hypothesis. Expect preterite overuse; give early preterite-only lessons, introduce the imperfect separately (the app already does this, imperfecto in phase 7), and test the contrast with context sentences.
- Subjunctive: explicit instruction has weak effects at intermediate level, so keep it B1–B2, frequent and short, with input-based tasks (cloze with context, true/false on mood) rather than rule recitation.
- Clitics (lo/la): use structured-input activities (matching a sentence to a meaning, true/false) before production, which is supported by VanPatten & Cadierno 1993 for object pronouns (the lo/la case itself is in this target-structure family).

### Gaps
- Original VanPatten 1987 article not seen (title, journal UNVERIFIED). Geeslin 2003 volume/pages not seen.
- Comajoan, Gudmestad, Montrul, Alarcón (gender agreement), por/para acquisition, article use by Slavic (article-less L1) learners, and L2 Spanish phonology (stress, vowels, taps/trills): no results this pass; UNVERIFIED. Needs a separate search.
- Primary Andersen & Shirai and Salaberry (1999/2000) papers not retrieved.

## 2. Cognitive / operative pedagogical grammar (Ruiz Campillo, Llopis-García, Castañeda, CVC dictionary)

### Takeaway
The "declarar / no declarar" account of indicative vs subjunctive is real and documented (Ruiz Campillo, 2007; marcoELE teaching activities). The 2012 Edinumen book is confirmed in the earlier note. Details of the Gramática básica, Castañeda Castro, and the CVC dictionary could not be verified, partly because CVC was unreachable.

### Cited Findings
- Ruiz Campillo's model identifies indicative with "declaración" and subjunctive with "no declaración": the indicative asserts or presupposes information as holding, the subjunctive does not declare it. — [Ruiz Campillo, "El concepto de no-declaración como valor del subjuntivo" (PDF hosted on todoele)](https://todoele.net/sites/default/files/2026-01/Campillo-Elconceptodenodeclaracionmunich.pdf) (title confirmed by the search result; I did not read the paper)
- marcoELE teaching material built on the model: Yanguas Santos, "Declarar o no declarar; esa es la cuestión" (uses afirmar/suponer as declaring and cuestionar as not declaring). — [marcoELE PDF](https://marcoele.com/descargas/7/yanguas-declarar.pdf)
- Molina-Vidal, "¿Indicativo o subjuntivo?", a digital activity on cognitive grammar citing Ruiz Campillo 2007 on declaración/no declaración. — [Nebrija journal PDF (as listed in results)](https://revistas.nebrija.com/revista-linguistica/article/download/3/6/13) (this URL's article identity is uncertain; check before citing)
- A thesis (Lozano 2007) has a section "Declarar (indicativo) o no declarar (subjuntivo)" applying it. — [UAB repository](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/trerecpro/2007/hdl_2072_4331/L_Lozano_Tesina_Julio_de_2007.pdf)
- A 2021 edition of *Gramática básica del estudiante de español* lists Ruiz Campillo as co-author (author list and ISBN not confirmed). — [search snippet, see Lozano/others above]; the earlier note also did not verify it.
- Llopis-García, Real Espinosa & Ruiz Campillo (2012), *¿Qué gramática enseñar, qué gramática aprender?*, Edinumen: see `curricula_textbooks_sla.md` §3 (bibliographic record already confirmed there).
- Castañeda Castro: not found (UNVERIFIED). CVC *Diccionario de términos clave de ELE*: not verified (CVC down; UNVERIFIED).

### Inferences
- Tip wording for mood: "indicativo = lo presento como información declarada; subjuntivo = no lo declaro (no lo afirmo, lo valoro, lo deseo, lo dudo)". Short, testable with find-the-mistake and true/false tasks, and consistent with the operative approach. Show one meaning per form, not a list of triggers.
- Treat this as a pedagogical model (a teaching proposal), not an empirical result; the SLA evidence in §1 says instruction on mood is hard to make effective regardless of model.

### Gaps
- Castañeda Castro's cognitive-grammar works, the CVC term dictionary entry, Alonso Raya et al. authorship/ISBN: not verified.
- No empirical test of the declarar/no declarar approach was found in this pass.

## 3. Vocabulary, memory and instruction research

### Takeaway
Confirmed: Nation 2022 (3rd ed.), Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010 (95%/98% coverage), FSRS-related papers by Ye et al. (KDD 2022 with DOI; TKDE 2023 with DOI), Bjork & Bjork 2011, Rohrer & Taylor 2007, VanPatten & Cadierno 1993 and Norris & Ortega 2000. Not found: Karpicke reviews beyond the already covered 2006 paper, Swain output hypothesis, R. Ellis principles, Long focus on form, Plonsky meta-analysis, VanPatten 2004 volume (UNVERIFIED).

### Cited Findings
- Nation, I. S. P. (2022). *Learning Vocabulary in Another Language* (3rd ed.), Cambridge University Press (Cambridge Applied Linguistics). Hardback ISBN 9781009098359; paperback 9781009096171; EPUB 9781009103282; published 9 June 2022; pages listed as 630 or 640. — [Cambridge front matter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810090/98359/frontmatter/9781009098359_frontmatter.pdf); [LINGUIST List announcement](https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/linguist/2022-October/109839.html); [Schweitzer listing](https://www.schweitzer-online.de/en/buch/Nation/Learning-Vocabulary-Another-Language/9781009098359/A63409586/)
- Laufer, B., & Ravenhorst-Kalovski, G. C. (2010). Lexical threshold revisited: Lexical text coverage, learners' vocabulary size and reading comprehension. *Reading in a Foreign Language* 22(1), 15–30. Minimal threshold: 95% coverage (about 4,000–5,000 word families); optimal: 98% (about 8,000 families), figures include proper nouns. Small gains in vocabulary still improve comprehension. These counts are for English texts. A 2025 preprint argues the evidence does not support a sharp coverage threshold. — [ScholarSpace PDF](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstream/10125/66648/1/22_1_10125_66648_laufer.pdf); [NFLRC record](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item/206)
- Ye, J., Su, J., & Cao, Y. (2022). A Stochastic Shortest Path Algorithm for Optimizing Spaced Repetition Scheduling. *Proceedings of the 28th ACM SIGKDD Conference (KDD '22)*, pp. 4381–4390. DOI 10.1145/3534678.3539081. Built from 220 million learner logs (MaiMemo); the SSP-MMC scheduler minimises review cost. — [dblp record](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/kdd/YeSC22.html); [MaiMemo repo](https://github.com/maimemo/ssp-mmc)
- Su, J., Ye, J., Nie, L., Cao, Y., & Chen, Y. (2023). Optimizing spaced repetition schedule by capturing the dynamics of memory. *IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering*, DOI 10.1109/TKDE.2023.3251721 (volume and pages not retrieved). — same search result (record from the KDD DOI search; open the DOI to confirm). Note: the author order differs from the task brief ("Ye, Su, Cao et al."); KDD 2022 authors are Ye, Su, Cao. Whether FSRS (the algorithm in ts-fsrs) is formally published in these papers is NOT established: FSRS builds on this research line, but the two papers describe SSP-MMC and DHP memory models. Treat the FSRS link as UNVERIFIED.
- Bjork, E. L., & Bjork, R. A. (2011). Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way: Creating desirable difficulties to enhance learning. In M. A. Gernsbacher et al. (Eds.), *Psychology and the Real World*, Worth Publishers, pp. 56–64. Helpful difficulties include spacing, interleaving, testing, varying conditions; not every difficulty is desirable. — [Bjork lab PDF](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)
- Rohrer, D., & Taylor, K. (2007). The shuffling of mathematics problems improves learning. *Instructional Science* 35, 481–498. One week after practice, shuffled (interleaved) practice greatly outperformed blocked practice; a 2014 follow-up (Rohrer, Dedrick & Burgess) found benefits even for less similar problem types. This is maths, not language, so transfer to Spanish is an inference. — [USF Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1767/); [PDF copy](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2007-rohrer.pdf)
- Norris, J. M., & Ortega, L. (2000). Effectiveness of L2 instruction: A research synthesis and quantitative meta-analysis. *Language Learning* 50(3), 417–528. 49 unique samples, studies 1980–1998. Instruction produced large gains on targeted forms, explicit outperformed implicit, focus on form and focus on forms had comparable effects, gains durable; critics question the coding and the equal-effect finding. Read from abstract and commentary only. — [DeepDyve abstract record](https://www.deepdyve.com/lp/wiley/does-type-of-instruction-make-a-difference-substantive-findings-from-a-9dzVAhpcHv/); [commentary](https://journals.library.columbia.edu/index.php/SALT/article/view/1530)
- VanPatten & Cadierno 1993 and VanPatten & Oikkenon 1996: see §1 (input processing and structured input).

### Inferences
- New words per lesson: no number was found in this pass, so the app cannot cite one. Nation's book is the right place to look for guidance on learning load; read it before fixing a lesson size.
- Coverage: the 95%/98% figures support giving learners the most frequent words first, but they refer to English reading and word families; do not reuse them as Spanish counts.
- FSRS: cite Ye et al. 2022 only for the general case that data-driven scheduling reduces review cost; do not claim it validates FSRS itself.
- Mixed lessons (interleaved tenses, ser/estar) are justified by Rohrer & Taylor plus Bjork & Bjork (spacing and interleaving are desirable difficulties), with the caveat of transfer from maths. Guided path can start blocked for a new form and mix afterwards.
- Norris & Ortega support explicit grammar tips plus practice; they do not tell us which tip wording is best.

### Gaps
- Karpicke reviews (beyond Roediger & Karpicke 2006), Swain output hypothesis, R. Ellis instructed-SLA principles, Long focus on form, Plonsky meta-analyses of L2 instruction, VanPatten (Ed.) 2004 *Processing Instruction / Theories in SLA*: not searched or not found (UNVERIFIED).
- The Nation chapter on learning load and per-lesson number of words: not read.
- FSRS algorithm primary reference (open-spaced-repetition documentation): not searched.

## 4. Pronunciation references (Hualde, Schwegler et al., Navarro Tomás, Mexican features)

### Takeaway
Hualde 2005 and Schwegler et al. are confirmed as books; edition details differ and need care. For Mexican features, only one peer-reviewed source (Dabkowski on Mexico City vowel reduction) was found, plus a university web page; seseo and intonation of Mexico City were not sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Hualde, J. I. (2005). *The Sounds of Spanish*. Cambridge University Press. Paperback ISBN 978-0-521-54538-9 (ISBN-10 0-521-54538-2, listed with an audio CD); hardback ISBN 978-0-521-83688-3 (listed by a retailer, marked unavailable); a later reissue ISBN 978-1-108-81788-2. — [Cambridge front matter](https://assets.cambridge.org/052154/5382/frontmatter/0521545382_frontmatter.htm); [LINGUIST List announcement](https://listserv.linguistlist.org/pipermail/linguist/2006-September/032544.html)

The 5th edition (2019, ISBN 978-1-118-74477-2, 416 pp) lists Schwegler and Ameal-Guerra (Kempff no longer credited in the listing). The 3rd edition (ISBN 978-0-471-46649-9) is listed with different authors. Latest edition therefore appears to be the 5th (2019), per retailer listings only. — [BooksRun 5th ed.](https://booksrun.com/9781118744772-fonetica-y-fonologia-espanolas-textbook-5th-edition); [BiggerBooks 4th ed.](https://www.biggerbooks.com/fontica-y-fonologa-espaolas-4th-edition/bk/9780470421925)
- Dabkowski, M. (see record), A sociophonetic investigation of Mexico City Spanish vowel reduction, John Benjamins (Issues in Hispanic and Lusophone Linguistics? the series code in the URL is rllt.16; year and series name UNVERIFIED). Finding: unstressed vowels weaken more than stressed; two strategies, voice weakening (all vowels, mostly post-tonic) and shortening (high vowels and /o/, mostly pre-tonic and in unstressed monosyllables); /a/ resists weakening most. — [Benjamins record](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/rllt.16.07dab); [OSU speech lab page](https://u.osu.edu/speechlabsppo/vowelweakening)
- Central Mexico: syllable-final /s/ is rarely weakened; weakening is mainly coastal and peripheral. This is from a general web page, not a scholarly source. — [Newcastle page "Mexican Spanish"](https://www.staff.ncl.ac.uk/i.e.mackenzie/mexican.htm) (low reliability)
- Navarro Tomás (*Manual de pronunciación española*): not searched (UNVERIFIED). Lipski's Latin American Spanish volume: not found (UNVERIFIED).

### Inferences
- A learner-facing audio standard of "clear central-Mexican Spanish, /s/ pronounced, unstressed vowels may shorten" is consistent with the sources, but treat vowel reduction as listening exposure, not as something to teach production of.
- For tips on stress, vowels and taps/trills, Hualde (2005) is a usable citation once the relevant chapters are read.

### Gaps
- Seseo in Mexico, Mexico City intonation, a scholarly source for /s/ retention (the Newcastle page is not scholarly), Navarro Tomás, Lipski: not verified.
- Dabkowski's full citation (first name, year, series): to be completed from the Benjamins page.
