# Mexican usage points, PCIC level mapping, ELE textbooks: gap-filling pass

Method note. About 21 tool calls (WebSearch and WebFetch). Status labels: CONFIRMED = a catalogue, publisher or journal page with bibliographic data was seen. SNIPPET = seen only in a search-result summary, full text not opened. UNVERIFIED = background knowledge only, do not cite. The CVC site (cvc.cervantes.es) still redirects to comunes.cervantes.es/mantenimiento, and web.archive.org is blocked for the fetch tool. Because of that, the exact PCIC level of each grammar point could NOT be read from the primary text (see question 3).

## 1. Mexican usage points: scholarly sources

### Takeaway
Scholarship was found for most points: mande (Zieliński 2022; Hernández Páez 2020 thesis), -le forms (Signos Lingüísticos article; Purdue thesis on órale), ir a vs. synthetic future (Lastra and Martín Butragueño 2010), tú/usted (Textos en Proceso 2018 and others), refusals and politeness (Félix-Brasdefer 2008), clitics (Moreno de Alba 2013). Nothing scholarly was found for nomás/ya or for ojalá/que + subjunctive in Mexico. For ahorita and diminutives no new sources were found beyond the existing notes.

### Cited Findings

#### ¿Mande?
- Zieliński, A. (2022). «No se dice "¿qué?", se dice "¿mande?"». Origen y evolución de la fórmula "mande" en español. *Romanica Cracoviensia* 22(2), 173–187 (Wydawnictwo UJ). CONFIRMED via the CEEOL page; no DOI shown. Finding: *mande* arose as a directive speech act, is attested in peninsular Spanish from the 16th century, and today is typical of Mexican Spanish, where with interrogative intonation it answers a call or asks for repetition. App use: backs a tip on ¿Mande? as a polite "¿Cómo?" and the point that it is Mexican-typical, not universal. — [CEEOL record](https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1088051)
- Hernández Páez, M. (2020), doctoral thesis, UAM-Iztapalapa, "La respuesta al llamado con mande en el español de México, un estudio de variación pragmático-discursiva". SNIPPET only (title, author and year taken from a search summary); a link to the thesis was among the results but was not opened. — [search hit, UAM Iztapalapa repository](https://bindani.izt.uam.mx/downloads/ws859f85j?locale=de)
- Company Company, C. (2022), UNAM note on her conference: calls the idea of a "sumamente educado" Mexican Spanish with many politeness formulas a stereotype; she describes a general effect of attenuation and distancing. SNIPPET (a PDF on colnal.mx; text not opened). — [colnal.mx PDF](https://colnal.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/ES-UN-ESTEREOTIPO-QUE-EL-ESPAÑOL-DE-MÉXICO-SEA-SUMAMENTE-EDUCADO-Y-CON-FÓRMULAS-DE-CORTESÍA_-CONCEPCIÓN-COMPANY-COMPANY_compressed.pdf)

#### -le intensifier (ándale, órale, híjole)
- A *Signos Lingüísticos* (UAM-Iztapalapa) article analyses the clitic *le* in *ándale* and *apúrale* as grammaticalization and pragmaticalization; the search summary says the phenomenon also occurs in Chile, Colombia, Venezuela and Argentina. SNIPPET: authors, year and title were not seen (the PDF link is below). Open the PDF before citing. — [Signos Lingüísticos PDF](https://signoslinguisticos.izt.uam.mx/index.php/SL/article/download/226/214)
- Purdue University dissertation, "The Discourse Marker Órale in Mexican Spanish: A Pragmatic and Sociolinguistic Approach", based on the Corpus Sociolingüístico de la Ciudad de México; proposes a core meaning. SNIPPET: author and year not seen. — [Purdue record](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI30505791)
- Origin of -le is disputed among popular sources (órale from *orar* + le, or from *ora/ahora* + le); these are Wikipedia, Wiktionary and the Chilango site, NOT scholarly. Do not cite them for etymology. Híjole: only the popular Chilango piece found; no scholarly source.

#### ahorita
- No new source beyond Granados (already in the earlier note). Not searched again.

#### ya / no más (nomás)
- Found no study of nomás. Nearby: papers on Mexican discourse markers *luego* (Guillén Escamilla, 2025, RAEL, mitigating value) and *de plano* (same author), both SNIPPET. A reasonable analogue only. — [RAEL article hit](https://matrix.aesla.org.es/RAEL/article/download/701/372/4644)

#### Periphrastic future ir a + inf. vs. synthetic future
- Lastra, Y. and Martín Butragueño, P. (2010). "Futuro morfológico y futuro perifrástico en el Corpus sociolingüístico de la ciudad de México." *Oralia* 13, 145–171. SNIPPET (citation as given in a search summary of a UNAM thesis; I did not open the article). Study of both futures in the CDMX corpus.
- Moreno de Alba (1970) is cited as an early Mexican source noting the decline of the synthetic future, especially in speech. SNIPPET (secondhand, via the same summaries; exact title not seen).
- A UNAM thesis on the periphrastic and morphological future in CDMX speech (norma culta vs. habla popular), and a UNAM thesis on schoolchildren's writing finding the synthetic form is a cultured register while the periphrasis is the commonest. SNIPPET; authors and titles not seen. — [UNAM thesis](https://ru.dgb.unam.mx/bitstream/20.500.14330/TES01000273223/3/273223.pdf); [UNAM thesis 2](https://ru.dgb.unam.mx/jspui/bitstream/20.500.14330/TES01000825530/3/0825530.pdf)
- Sobczak, in *Studia Iberystyczne*: literary corpus comparison of future forms in Spain and Mexico, 1929–2015. SNIPPET; title and year not seen. — [Studia Iberystyczne PDF](https://journals.akademicka.pl/si/article/download/2531/2395)
- App use: supports "in speech, say voy a hablar; use the -ré future for formal writing, promises and probability". Needs one opened study before a numeric claim.

#### Tú/usted address and politeness
- Félix-Brasdefer, J. C. (2008). *Politeness in Mexico and the United States: A contrastive study of the realization and perception of refusals.* Amsterdam: John Benjamins, Pragmatics & Beyond New Series 171, xiv + 195 pp. Print ISBN 9789027254153, e-book 9789027291448. CONFIRMED (publisher page and library records). Finding (from the search summary of related work): Mexicans used more indirect refusal strategies than the comparison group, tied to preference for independence face. — [Benjamins](https://www.benjamins.com/catalog/pbns.171)
- Félix-Brasdefer (2008), "Sociopragmatic variation: Dispreferred responses in Mexican and Dominican Spanish", *Journal of Politeness Research* 4(1)? The DOI is 10.1515/PR.2008.004, so the journal is *Pragmatics and Beyond? / Journal of Politeness Research* (journal name and volume UNVERIFIED; the DOI prefix PR suggests *Journal of Politeness Research*). — [De Gruyter record](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/PR.2008.004/pdf)
- Félix-Brasdefer on Mexican requests: Mexican participants preferred positive politeness markers, diminutives and interrogatives, while Spaniards preferred imperatives. SNIPPET; the exact paper was not identified. App use: backs "Mexican requests are softened (¿me podrías…?, un momentito)". Verify before citing.
- Félix-Brasdefer, 2006, *Journal of Pragmatics*: politeness and face in male Mexican speakers' refusals in formal and informal contexts. SNIPPET; the title and pages were not seen.
- tú/usted in Mexico City: an article in *Textos en Proceso* (UNAM), 2018, 48 speakers by sex, age and education, concludes that tuteo cannot be confirmed as the unmarked form in the capital; it notes the last large studies are Lastra 1972 and Kim Lee 1989. SNIPPET: authors, volume and pages were not seen. — [UNAM thesis/article list](https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/1809/2568/4249) (this link is the Anuario de Letras item; the 2018 article's own link was not identified)
- Orozco (2026), *Anuario de Letras*: generalizing pronouns *uno*, *tú*, *usted* in 12 interviews of the Norma culta CDMX corpus. SNIPPET; first name and pages not seen. — [Anuario de Letras PDF](https://revistas-filologicas.unam.mx/anuario-letras/index.php/al/article/download/1809/2568/4249)
- Cepeda Ruiz (2024), *Inflexiones* (UNAM): (des)cortesía in the corpus El habla popular de la Ciudad de México. SNIPPET. — [Inflexiones PDF](https://inflexiones.unam.mx/index.php/inflexiones/article/download/198/215/607)
- Note: the evidence says the capital is not simply "tuteo"; the app default (tú for friends, usted for older people and service contexts) is a teaching simplification, not a research result.

#### lo/le (no leísmo)
- Moreno de Alba, J. G. (2013), article in *Anuario de Letras* (the Elsevier page is the Anuario de Letras Lingüística y Filología, vol. 1 issue 3? NOT confirmed): shows the plural "se los dije" for "se lo dije" is typical of American and especially Mexican Spanish; in CREA, "se los dije" has 20 records, 13 from Mexico. Also, in an earlier note he says Mexican Spanish has no loísmo or laísmo and only limited leísmo. SNIPPET: title and volume not seen. — [Anuario de Letras (Elsevier)](https://www.elsevier.es/es-revista-anuario-letras-linguistica-filologia-73-pdf-download-S0185137313710310)
- A UNAM thesis has a section "¿Leísmo en México?", treating it as an open question; Gutiérrez found a slight leísmo trend in Houston, stronger than in Mexico City. SNIPPET. — [UNAM thesis](https://ru.dgb.unam.mx/bitstream/20.500.14330/TES01000211797/3/0211797.pdf)
- Caution: the "se los dije" pattern is considered nonstandard by prescriptive grammars. SOMOS should not teach it; it only matters for explaining what learners hear.

#### Mexican vs. Spain lexicon
- Moreno de Alba, J. G. (1992). *Diferencias léxicas entre España y América*. Madrid: MAPFRE, Colección Idioma e Iberoamérica, 291 pp. CONFIRMED as MAPFRE, not FCE (library records; the NRFH review describes it as a synthesis limited to lexicon). — [Nantes catalogue](https://nantilus.univ-nantes.fr/vufind/Record/PPN012692085); [NRFH review](https://nrfh.colmex.mx/index.php/nrfh/article/download/2649/2614)
- VARILEX (Variación léxica del español en el mundo), led by Hiroto Ueda (University of Tokyo); data from 61 Spanish-speaking cities; Varilex-R is the revised database by Ueda and F. Moreno Fernández. CONFIRMED via the Heidelberg project page. Also Ueda, H. and Ruiz Tinoco, A. (2019), Iberoamericana Vervuert (SNIPPET: exact title not seen). — [Heidelberg HCIAS](https://hcias.uni-heidelberg.de/en/research-at-the-hcias/ongoing-projects-at-the-hcias/variacion-lexica-en-espanol-del-mundo-varilex-r); [Ueda 2005 PDF](https://www.unioviedo.es/felixfer/Facsimiles/Ueda_2005_VARILEX.pdf)

### Inferences
- mande, the future vs. periphrasis, and clitic points have enough sources for a "see sources" line in a tip. Written as "observed usage, check with a native speaker": nomás, ándale/órale, híjole, ojalá.
- The tú/usted evidence is mixed. The app should not claim Mexico is "more formal" in general (cf. Company Company).

### Gaps
- No scholarly source for nomás/ya, híjole, ojalá/que + subjunctive in Mexico. Authors/years of the Signos Lingüísticos -le article, the Purdue thesis and the Textos en Proceso 2018 article were not captured.
- Most items marked SNIPPET need the PDF opened.

## 4. ELE textbooks (Mexican / Latin American)

### Takeaway
UNAM CEPE has its own textbook series, and its programme has nine levels with CEFR-linked certification, but the CEFR mapping per level was not found. The US series are confirmed as existing in current editions, but nothing confirms a specifically Latin American or Mexican focus.

### Cited Findings
- UNAM CEPE programme "Español y cultura mexicana": nine levels, 126 hours each; certification tests four skills and refers to the CEFR levels. The 2026 CEPE Polanco calendar groups levels as Básicos 0–4, Intermedios 5–7, Avanzados 8 (nine levels, 0 to 8). An older institutional document mentions an eight-level plan, so the structure changed over time. — [UNAM CEPE description PDF](https://fu-berlin.de/sites/internationalweek/partneruniversitaeten/_dokumente-partneruniverstaeten/UNAM-CEPE---Espanol.pdf); [CEPE Polanco calendar 2026](https://www.cepepolanco.unam.mx/3_(Esp)_Calendario_completo_2026.pdf)
- CEPE certification exams: three levels Independiente (B1), Avanzado (B2), Competente (C1) in CELA; the same B1/B2/C1 correspondence is stated in the CELA guides. — [CELA Independiente guide](https://certificacion.cepe.unam.mx/cela/GUIA_CELA_INDEPENDIENTE_junio%202015.pdf); [CELA Avanzado](https://certificacion.cepe.unam.mx/cela/GUIA_CELA_AVANZADO_junio%202015.pdf); [CELA Competente](https://certificacion.cepe.unam.mx/cela/GUIA_CELA_COMPETENTE_junio%202015.pdf)
- CEPE textbook titles (SNIPPET from UNAM Memoria and humanindex): *Así hablamos* (first two books with audio, UNAM Memoria 2007); *¡Estoy listo!* (Cortés and Serrano, level one, with cassette; humanindex record); *Dicho y hecho* adapted to a digital version per the UNAM Memoria 2020. The Memoria 2020 mention of "Dicho y hecho" as a CEPE-adapted series is odd, since Dicho y hecho is also a US Wiley textbook; do not assume they are the same product. "Dimensiones" and "Mosaico" were NOT found as CEPE titles; drop them. — [humanindex ¡Estoy listo!](https://www.humanindex.unam.mx/humanindex/fichas_pdf/Libro6075%20Cortes%20ySerrano%20%20Estoy%20Listo%20%20nivel%20uno.pdf); [UNAM Memoria 2020 CEPE](https://www.planeacion.unam.mx/Memoria/2020/PDF/14.12-CEPE.pdf); [UNAM Memoria 2007](https://www.planeacion.unam.mx/unam40/2007/PDF/613.pdf)

- For Mexican scope-and-sequence checking, CEPE's own series (Así hablamos, ¡Estoy listo!) are the only found Mexico-made options; they need a catalogue lookup (UNAM Press / CEPE).
